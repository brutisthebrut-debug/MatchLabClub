# MatchLab Club: Marketing Handoff Brief

This is the single brief for whoever takes MatchLab to market. It explains what the
product actually is, how a stranger becomes a ready, matchable member, what to
promote first, and the guidance for launching city by city. Read it once before
writing a single ad.

## The one-line promise

Become genuinely relationship-ready, then get matched (AI-driven) with people you
would never find on your own, near you.

Readiness leads. Matching is the reward. Everything else is in service of those two.

## The spine: readiness first, matching is the payoff

There is one rising meter, **Match Readiness**. Every tool a member uses, every
source they connect, every quiz they answer feeds that one meter. The meter gates
matching: when a member crosses the readiness threshold for their city, they become
eligible for introductions.

The unifying line for all copy is: **the more the machine knows you, the better it
matches you.**

This matters for marketing because it tells you how to frame every feature. The
profile audits, Signal Score, message coaching, bio and prompt rewrites, and quizzes
are NOT the headline. They do two jobs at once:

1. Immediate value that keeps people sticky (they help you win on the apps you
   already use).
2. The way the machine gathers signal to understand a person.

Do not reposition the "optimize your existing dating apps" tools as the headline
product. They are the on-ramp. The headline is readiness leading to matching.

## The signal lanes (what the machine learns from)

Readiness is built from a living set of signal lanes. Each lane is one entry in the
signal registry, so the list grows over time without breaking the meter. Today the
lanes a member can feed include:

- Profile and bio signals (audits, bio and prompt rewrites)
- Message and conversation signals (message coaching, communication patterns)
- Quiz and self-reflection signals (attachment style and the other quizzes)
- Date outcome signals (what happened after a date: another date, no more, ghosted,
  unsure) which now quietly teach the engine which signals predict a real connection
- Calendar rhythm (a pasted .ics gives a derived cadence, never the raw events)
- Connector signals on the roadmap (forwarding inbox for receipts, spending signals,
  social tone) each surfaced in the Connection Center with a clear "what we will see
  and never touch" contract

Marketing only needs the shape: more lanes fed means a higher readiness score and a
sharper match. Members can always remove any source and purge its data.

## Privacy promise (say this plainly, it is a selling point)

Two layers of AI. A deterministic engine runs on every account by default with no
keys, no external calls, and no rate limits. On top of that, Anthropic Claude is
opt-in per account through a single toggle. When the toggle is off, or a Claude call
fails, the deterministic engine handles the request and nothing breaks.

We never sell, share, or train on member content. Only derived counts and scores are
ever sent to the AI layer, never raw messages, photos, or personal details. Members
can export and delete everything at any time.

Do not write copy that says "no external AI" without qualification. The honest line
is: deterministic by default, opt-in Claude for depth, zero retention, nothing sold
or trained on.

## The funnels

### Quizzes (top of funnel)

Quizzes are the lightest, most shareable entry point. The **Attachment Style** quiz
is the featured, start-here quiz on the quizzes page and should be the primary quiz
in paid and organic promotion. It pairs with the blog post on attachment styles for
a natural read-then-act loop. Every quiz now carries its own icon and ends with a
result plus a CTA back into the product, so a quiz taker has a clear next step toward
readiness.

Promote the Attachment Style quiz first. It is the cleanest hook: high curiosity,
universally relatable, and it immediately starts feeding the readiness meter.

### Blog (organic and trust)

The blog is a stickiness and SEO lever. Posts should end with a CTA into the matching
tool or a relevant quiz so reading converts into signal. Keep the voice human and
inclusive.

### Tools (value that earns the data)

The audit, Signal Score, message coach, and bio and prompt rewrites are the proof of
value. Lead a skeptic in with a free tool, show them an immediate win, then frame the
readiness climb as the reason to keep going.

### Pricing and waitlist

Three tiers: Free ($0), Dating Reset ($97), Wingman ($197 per month). A podcast promo
code and an early-listener waitlist perk are in place. The waitlist shows a live count
and is the right CTA in any city that has not yet reached matching density.

## Cold-start and city density (read before any launch)

Matching is radius-based (for example 25, 35, or 45 miles) and only turns on once a
city has enough ready members to make introductions worth a member's time. Until then,
the honest move is to grow the waitlist and the readiness climb in that city, not to
promise live matches.

Guidance:

- In a new city, lead with the quizzes, the free tools, and the waitlist. Sell the
  readiness climb and the privacy promise. Be explicit that matching unlocks once the
  city has depth.
- The product already tells members how many people in their city are building a match
  profile. Use that honestly in local copy. Scarcity and momentum are real here, do not
  fake them.
- Concentrate spend per city rather than spreading thin nationally. Density in one
  radius beats a handful of members scattered across the country.

## What is intentionally not the story (yet)

These exist as long-term direction, do not lead with them and do not promise them:

- An invite-only "after dark" mode for casual connection.
- AI date-idea and location suggestions within both people's vicinity.
- Full OAuth integrations with the dating apps.

## Inclusivity is non-negotiable

MatchLab is for all genders and orientations, inclusive by default. It is not a niche
app and must never be marketed as one. Every persona, image, and example should reflect
that.

## Voice rules (hard)

- No em dashes anywhere in member-facing copy.
- No AI-tell filler words (for example "dive in", "unleash", "elevate", "in today's
  world").
- No emojis in the product UI. The product uses clean iconography instead.
- Inclusive language always.

## What to promote first (the short answer)

1. The Attachment Style quiz as the hook.
2. The readiness climb and the privacy promise as the reason to stay.
3. A free tool win (audit or message coach) as the proof.
4. The waitlist in any city that has not reached matching density, with matching framed
   as the payoff that unlocks once the city has depth.
